    Season 12 Phase 4 Vote, and What's Next For Playoffs Qualification

    Details on the Season 12 phase 4 poll, and our plans for how qualification might change for Season 13.

    November 22, 2026 6 minutes read
    Table of contents
    1. The History of The Phase Point System
    2. A Solution: Phase-Peak Elo
    3. The Future of Playoffs Formats

    Hello everyone! Earlier this season, we announced that the Season 12 phase point system will feature some changes from previous seasons. Phases 1 and 3 would still use the traditional end-of-phase Elo phase point system, phase 2 would use a new phase-peak Elo phase point system, and the community would choose between these two systems to use in phase 4 through a community poll.

    Starting on 2026-11-23 00:00 UTC until 2026-11-28 00:00 UTC, all players with a Season 12 peak Elo at or above 1800 will have the option to vote in the community poll. The poll itself will just be a Google Form, posted in the MCSR Ranked Discord server. Shortly after the poll closes, the anonymized results will be published, and whichever system has more votes will be used for phase 4.

    📝 Note

    If you wish to submit a response without using the Google Form, or want to query the state of or change your response, please open a ticket in the MCSR Ranked Discord server. Invalid or fraudulent responses will be scanned for and culled. Some players may be given early access to submit their responses.

    The History of The Phase Point System

    Prior to Season 4, the top 8 players on the leaderboard at the end of each season would qualify directly to the Playoffs bracket. This system was simple, but flawed. Elo and leaderboard positions were (and still are) quite volatile, so a single snapshot of the leaderboard didn’t always provide an accurate ranking of the best players. The phase point system was introduced to fix this issue, among a few others.

    Since Season 5, each season has been split into four, month long phases.1 At the end of each phase, the top 100 players on the leaderboard would receive phase points, with higher ranking players earning significantly more phase points. At the end of the season, the top 12 players based on phase points would qualify for the Playoffs bracket. Essentially, this system averages out top ranked players’ leaderboard positions over a season, meaning Playoffs qualification was no longer based on a single snapshot of the volatile Elo leaderboard.

    Other than a few tweaks over the years, the phase point system has remained very similar to its original implementation in Season 4. In this time though, cracks have been starting to show. For example, like how the pre-Season 4 system only took a single snapshot of the leaderboard each season, the phase point system only takes four snapshots, which isn’t much better. Top players often lose motivation to play at the start of each phase, or don’t queue later in the phase to avoid the risk of losing.

    A Solution: Phase-Peak Elo

    A popular solution to the problems with this phase point system was proposed by the community: instead of awarding phase points based on end-of-phase Elo, award them based on the highest Elo rating achieved during the phase, or phase-peak Elo. The phase-peak system promised to fix many issues of the end-of-phase system for several reasons. First and foremost, the phase-peak system doesn’t just look at a few snapshots of the volatile leaderboard since players can reach a peak Elo at any point in the phase. Second, once a player is below their phase-peak Elo, losing an individual match doesn’t directly result in earning less phase points for the phase, which removes much of the reason why top players may choose not to queue later in the phase.

    For Season 12, we were hesitant to adopt the phase-peak system fully. Highly ranked players that are no longer attempting to set a new phase-peak Elo do not have Playoffs qualification as an incentive to win their matches, but they are still able to queue players that do have that incentive. Having “serious” players queue against “non-serious” players is not a problem unique to the phase-peak system, but it makes it far more obvious when it occurs. This issue, among a few others, combined with the fact that it was simply a new system that could have unknown issues, was enough to dissuade us from fully committing to the phase-peak system for Season 12. We still wanted to mix things up though, and this idea was popular, hence our decision to try it out just for phase 2, and potentially phase 4. We would be the first to admit that this alternating system isn’t ideal, but we wanted to try something new.

    As for the community vote: we didn’t want to commit to using the phase-peak system for phase 4, in case it didn’t work out in phase 2, and giving the community the choice to pick their preferred system seemed fitting. Originally, the poll was going to be open to all Ranked players, but this was clearly unpopular with the community, per feedback and discussion following the initial announcement. So, we’ve changed course to only allow players with some likelihood to earn phase points to participate, which we’ve decided to be a Season 12 peak Elo of 1800 or higher.

    The Future of Playoffs Formats

    The phase-peak system was somewhat successful in phase 2, but it also just didn’t change much compared to the flawed end-of-phase system. Ultimately, both these systems are flawed for the same reason: in-season Ranked matches are the primary way to qualify for Playoffs, but these matches aren’t held to nearly the same standard as Playoffs matches (by the players, community, and Ranked staff). Of course people care less about a random Ranked queue than a Playoffs match, but this frequently leads to anti-competitive or just noncompetitive behavior directly influencing who qualifies for Playoffs.

    To be clear, I don’t think the existing systems qualify the “wrong” players, in fact I think the existing qualification systems have done an excellent job at selecting the most deserving and skilled players. But simply selecting the best players is not the only goal of the Playoffs qualification system, or even Playoffs as a whole. Qualifying to Playoffs is also supposed to be a goal for the most ambitious Ranked players to strive for, and an incentive to continue improving at speedrunning Minecraft. For this to be the case though, the journey to achieve that goal needs to be fun and rewarding. It should feel fair and objective, and not unnecessarily tedious. It should be entertaining to viewers to watch and engage with a player’s journey to qualify, which necessitates that the player is having fun themselves.

    With all that being said, our intention for the Season 13 Playoffs qualification format is for it to be event based, rather than leaderboard based. Throughout the season, there will be several qualification events, and the top performers in these events are who qualify to Playoffs. There will likely still be some reliance on the leaderboard, for example it’s possible that only players above a certain place on the leaderboard will be eligible to participate in a qualification event, or these may be seeded using in-season Elo. But the goal is that tournaments host the last few matches that carry the weight of Playoffs qualification, not just random in-season Ranked queues.

    Expect another blog post in the coming weeks talking more about the upcoming Playoffs qualification changes.
